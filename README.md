---

1. Objective

To perform a complete pharmacovigilance assessment of a suspected adverse drug reaction (ADR), including case identification, ADR assessment, seriousness and severity classification, causality assessment, dechallenge assessment, and preparation of an Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR).

---

2. Simulated Case Study

A 28-year-old female patient was diagnosed with acute bacterial tonsillitis and was prescribed amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily.

The patient had no known previous drug allergies and was not taking any other regular medicines.

After taking amoxicillin for approximately 2 days, the patient developed:

- Generalized red, raised, itchy skin lesions
- Urticarial rash over the trunk and upper limbs
- Mild facial itching
- No difficulty in breathing
- No swelling of the tongue or throat
- No loss of consciousness
- No hospitalization

The patient contacted her physician. Amoxicillin was discontinued and an antihistamine was administered.

The urticarial lesions began to improve within 24 hours and completely resolved over the following few days.

No rechallenge with amoxicillin was performed.

8. Dechallenge Assessment

Dechallenge: Positive

After discontinuation of amoxicillin, the patient's urticaria improved and subsequently resolved.

This temporal improvement after withdrawal provides supportive evidence for a relationship between amoxicillin exposure and the reaction.

---

9. Rechallenge Assessment

Rechallenge: Not performed

The patient was not re-exposed to amoxicillin after recovery.

A rechallenge is not required for a WHO-UMC Probable/Likely assessment.

10. Seriousness Assessment

The event is classified as:

NON-SERIOUS

Reason

The patient:

- Did not die
- Was not reported to be at risk of death
- Did not require inpatient hospitalization
- Did not have prolonged hospitalization
- Did not develop persistent or significant disability/incapacity
- Did not have a congenital anomaly
- Did not require intensive emergency intervention

Therefore, based on the information available in this simulated case, the event does not meet the standard serious adverse event criteria.

Important: Seriousness is different from severity. An event can be severe in intensity without meeting regulatory seriousness criteria.

---

11. Severity Assessment

Hartwig and Siegel Severity Assessment

Severity Grade: 2

Justification

The ADR required discontinuation/change of the suspected drug and additional treatment with an antihistamine, but there was:

- No hospitalization
- No prolonged hospital stay
- No intensive medical care
- No permanent harm
- No death

Therefore, the simulated case is classified as Grade 2 (mild-to-moderate clinical severity) under the Hartwig and Siegel approach.

17. Case Narrative

A 28-year-old female patient was prescribed amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily for acute bacterial tonsillitis. The patient had no known previous drug allergy and was not receiving any other regular medication.

Approximately two days after initiation of amoxicillin, the patient developed a generalized itchy urticarial rash involving the trunk and upper limbs, with mild facial itching. There was no reported difficulty in breathing, tongue or throat swelling, loss of consciousness, or hospitalization.

Following medical evaluation, amoxicillin was discontinued and an antihistamine was administered. The patient's symptoms began to improve within approximately 24 hours and subsequently resolved completely over the following few days.

No rechallenge with amoxicillin was performed.

Based on the temporal relationship, absence of a reported alternative drug cause, and positive response following withdrawal of the suspected drug, the relationship between amoxicillin and urticaria was assessed as Probable/Likely according to the WHO-UMC causality assessment approach.

The event was classified as non-serious based on the information available in this simulated case. The severity was assessed as Hartwig and Siegel Grade 2 because treatment with the suspected drug was discontinued and symptomatic treatment was provided without hospitalization, intensive care, permanent harm, or death.

The patient recovered completely.

19. Pharmacovigilance Recommendations

For a real-world case, the Drug Safety Associate should:

- Verify patient and reporter information.
- Verify the medicinal product and dosage details.
- Confirm the exact event onset date.
- Obtain relevant medical history.
- Check all concomitant medications.
- Document treatment provided for the reaction.
- Document dechallenge and rechallenge information.
- Assess seriousness and severity.
- Perform causality assessment.
- Assess expectedness against the applicable reference safety information.
- Enter the case into the applicable pharmacovigilance safety database.
- Perform medical review and quality control.
- Follow applicable regulatory reporting timelines and procedures.
- Follow up the reporter if important information is missing.

---

20. Key Learning Points

1. An ADR is a harmful and unintended response suspected to be associated with a medicinal product.
2. A complete ICSR requires adequate patient, reporter, suspected product, and event information.
3. Causality assessment evaluates the likelihood that the drug caused the event.
4. WHO-UMC provides standardized causality categories.
5. Positive dechallenge can support a causal relationship.
6. Rechallenge is not always required and may not be appropriate clinically.
7. Severity and seriousness are different concepts.
8. Seriousness is based on defined regulatory outcome/action criteria.
9. A Drug Safety Associate should document the assessment objectively and avoid unsupported conclusions.
10. Missing information should be followed up rather than assumed.
